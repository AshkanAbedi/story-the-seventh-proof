# The Seventh Proof / Story Outline

## Backstory

### Concept

**The Seventh Proof** is a nonlinear narrative mystery game and a modern reimagining of Mikhail Bulgakov's *The Master and Margarita*. It is set in London during the seven days leading up to a bitter UK General Election.

London appears to be divided between two powerful political movements. Their supporters argue constantly, each side blaming the other for the country's problems. But beneath this public conflict, both sides are connected to the same criminal network. Money is being moved through elite art institutions, religious charities, extremist groups, and political campaigns.

At the center of this story is Professor Woland, a world-famous mentalist and illusionist who arrives in London with his strange entourage. He has been hired to perform one show at the Royal Alliance Theatre on election eve. Soon after his arrival, people begin to disappear, impossible crimes occur, and strange supernatural events spread through the city.

Woland has not come to corrupt London. London is already corrupt. He has come to expose what is hidden behind people's public identities.

The story is experienced through several playable characters, and their stories are presented out of chronological order. Each character sees only part of the truth:

- **Kenneth Fletcher**, a detective investigating the disappearance of theatre director Arthur Pennington.
- **Arthur Pennington**, a wealthy and corrupt theatre director whose disappearance starts the main investigation.
- **Yusuf Tariq**, a respected philanthropist who helps control the criminal network behind the political conflict.
- **Margaret Spenceley**, a woman who refuses to believe that her missing partner is dead.
- **Zoey Fernandez**, Arthur's assistant, who is trapped between her private life and the secrets she has discovered.
- **Michael Astor**, an investigative journalist whose hidden laptop may contain proof of the entire conspiracy.

The game combines environmental investigation, puzzles, tense dialogue, clue reconstruction, and multiple perspectives. No single character knows the whole story. The player's job is to connect their discoveries and understand what really happened.

At its heart, **The Seventh Proof** is a mystery about people hiding behind masks. Political masks, charitable masks, religious masks, romantic masks, and finally the Devil's own mask as an entertainer. The supernatural horror is real, but the worst crimes in the story are committed by ordinary people.

### Theme

#### The Spine

Unlike Bulgakov's original novel, this story does not focus on Soviet atheism or defend religious faith against it. Instead, it uses the same idea of the Devil entering a corrupt society and applies it to our modern times.

Woland arrives in a city that has already lost much of its honesty. Institutions publicly claim to serve different causes, but privately they are driven by money, power, and self-interest. Political groups, charities, religious organizations, and cultural institutions may appear to stand for completely different values, yet some of them are connected by the same hidden financial system.

This is the central idea of the story:

> **The Devil is more honest than the institutions, because the Devil never pretends to be something else.**

Woland does not need to create corruption. He only needs to reveal it.

The election is the setting for this conflict. The story is not saying that both political sides are simply "equally bad." The deeper target is the loss of sincerity: people who stop questioning their beliefs, powerful people who pretend to believe in values they privately betray, and people who know something is wrong but are too afraid to act.

#### The Cardinal Sin: The Gap Between Truth and Performance

The story treats **dogmatism, hypocrisy, and cowardice** as different forms of the same failure: choosing a comfortable story over the truth.

**Dogmatism** means refusing to question one's beliefs. It is not the same as faith or political conviction. It is the moment when a person stops testing those beliefs against reality.

**Hypocrisy** means publicly supporting values that a person privately betrays. Arthur presents himself as a champion of culture and democracy while using his theatre for corruption. Yusuf presents himself as a devout philanthropist while using religion and charity to move dirty money. The political campaigns publicly attack one another while secretly benefiting from the same network.

**Cowardice** belongs to people who know enough to doubt the official story but choose safety over truth. They include editors who refuse to publish, officials who bury evidence, and ordinary people who accept the version of events that is easiest to live with.

All three create the same distance between a person's public face and their true self.

Against that dishonesty, the story places three sincere human values: **Truth, Love, and Courage**.

Woland repeatedly tests these values. He puts sincere people into situations where their choices have a real cost, while the people who built their lives on lies are forced to face the truth they tried to hide.

#### The Verdict

Woland's journey does not end with a simple punishment.

Instead, he offers certain people a final choice: **peace**.

Some characters are already exhausted by the world they live in and by the consequences of their choices. Woland can offer them a way out. The question is not simply who deserves punishment, but who is willing to face the truth and what each person chooses when there is no longer anywhere to hide.

**Who will choose peace, and who will remain behind?**

### The Election

London, one week before a contentious UK General Election, is full of political and ideological tension. Two movements dominate the public conversation.

- **The Concord Party**, led by **Diane Sterling**, is openly socialist and campaigns for open borders, managed markets, a larger state, higher taxes, and a strong opposition to nationalism. Its message is built around universal solidarity, but dissent is often treated as ignorance.
- **The New Albion Movement (NAM)**, led by **Edward Thorne**, is openly right-wing and campaigns for national sovereignty, controlled immigration, free enterprise, and open markets. Its message is built around national revival, but dissent is often treated as betrayal.

The public sees these movements as enemies. Their supporters believe they represent opposite visions for Britain.

Unknown to both the public and their most committed supporters, however, both campaigns receive money from the same hidden network.

Two major parts of that network are the **One Soul Charity Foundation** and the **Royal Alliance Theatre Association**. Money is moved through the two organizations and then returned to political campaigns through layers of false transactions.

The political conflict is real, but the people profiting from it are not on one side or the other.

### Arthur Pennington and Yusuf Tariq

#### Arthur Pennington

Arthur Pennington is the director of the Royal Alliance Theatre Association and one of London's wealthiest men.

To the public, he is a great supporter of culture and democracy. He presents himself as a self-made man who rose from nothing and believes that art should be available to everyone. He hosts glamorous events, raises money for the Concord Party, and knows how to make himself look generous in front of cameras.

In private, Arthur is very different.

He is vain, indulgent, and obsessed with luxury. He loves expensive drinks, beautiful art, women, masks, riddles, and locked rooms. His penthouse reflects his personality: almost everything important is protected by some kind of puzzle or secret.

Arthur genuinely loves theatre. That is one of the few things he truly believes in. But he has also turned the institution he loves into part of a money-laundering operation.

He hides his secrets so carefully that hiding them has become a source of pleasure.

#### Yusuf Tariq

Yusuf Tariq is the director of the One Soul Charity Foundation and Arthur's long-time partner in the criminal operation.

Where Arthur is emotional and theatrical, Yusuf is controlled and patient.

To the public, Yusuf is a respected philanthropist and devout man. He dresses simply, speaks softly, and appears beside religious leaders and politicians from different backgrounds. He raises money in the name of faith, charity, and community.

But Yusuf treats all of these things as tools.

One Soul receives donations and moves money through hidden channels that eventually support extremist groups and political operations. The more fear and conflict exist in the city, the more useful the system becomes.

Arthur creates the spectacle. Yusuf manages the system behind it.

### The Theatre Attack

A year before the events of the game, masked gunmen attacked a Royal Alliance branch theatre during a performance of Goethe's *Faust*.

The attack began in the middle of the performance. The audience initially thought the disturbance might be part of the show.

Then the shooting began.

Several people were killed. Others were taken through the theatre's service corridors and disappeared.

Among the dead were **Kenneth Fletcher's wife and five-year-old daughter**.

Also missing after the attack was investigative journalist **Michael Astor**, who was officially listed as "unaccounted for."

A fringe religious-extremist group claimed responsibility. The claim was quickly accepted by the press and politicians, and the case was taken over by counter-terrorism authorities.

But the investigation never produced a clear explanation.

There was no ransom demand. No confirmed mastermind was found. No surviving hostages were recovered. Eventually, much of the investigation disappeared into sealed files.

London moved on.

A year later, both political campaigns still use the attack as part of their election message. Thorne presents it as evidence of national decline, uncontrolled immigration, and the danger of extremism. Sterling presents it as evidence of the destruction caused by hatred and division.

Neither side knows that the same hidden network has profited from both narratives.

#### What Actually Happened — Hidden Canon

The attack was not an independent terrorist attack. It was a **false-flag operation** planned by Arthur Pennington and Yusuf Tariq.

They used an extremist cell that had secretly received money through the One Soul network. The group believed it was carrying out an attack for its own cause. It did not know that Arthur and Yusuf had planned the operation.

The attack was designed to achieve three goals.

**1. Create political conflict.**

The massacre gave both political movements a powerful story to use against the other. Every new argument increased fear, anger, and donations.

The conspiracy had created the conflict that it later used to make money.

**2. Weaken the police.**

An unsolved mass-casualty attack would overwhelm the police, damage public trust, and move the investigation into counter-terrorism. A police force focused on the attack and under political pressure would be less likely to investigate other crimes connected to Arthur and Yusuf.

**3. Capture Michael Astor.**

Michael was the real target.

He had discovered the hidden financial network and was close to proving how Arthur, Yusuf, the theatre, the charity, and the political campaigns were connected.

Arthur and Yusuf wanted him alive because they believed he was the only person who knew where his evidence was stored.

The attack was intended to be controlled. It was not.

The extremists killed more people than planned, turning the operation into a genuine massacre.

Michael survived and was taken away.

### Michael Astor and Margaret Spenceley

#### Michael Astor

Michael Astor is an investigative journalist who began looking into the finances of the Royal Alliance Theatre Association and the One Soul Charity Foundation two years before the story begins.

Working alone, he followed a chain of suspicious payments. He discovered fake art commissions, cryptocurrency transactions, political accounts, and other financial movements that pointed toward the same hidden network.

He stored his research on a personal laptop and kept it hidden.

Then the theatre attack happened.

Michael was abducted during the attack. Officially, he disappeared with the other missing people.

#### Hidden Canon: Michael's Fate

Michael is alive.

Arthur and Yusuf arranged for him to be placed under a false name and false medical diagnosis in a private psychiatric clinic funded by the One Soul Charity Foundation.

The clinic is his prison.

His records are protected by patient confidentiality, he is heavily sedated, and he has been kept isolated from the outside world. Arthur and Yusuf cannot simply kill him because they still need to find his laptop.

Michael also refuses to reveal where it is.

For a year, both sides have been trapped in the same stalemate: Michael will not give them the location, and they cannot risk killing him until they find it.

The experience has almost destroyed him, but whenever he is lucid, he continues looking for a way to escape.

#### Margaret Spenceley

Margaret is Michael's partner and the love of his life.

For a year, she has searched for him. She has followed missing-person reports, visited hospitals, attended vigils, and pursued every lead she could find.

Most people around her believe that Michael is dead.

Margaret does not.

She has no proof, but she cannot let go of the belief that Michael is still alive somewhere in London.

That certainty eventually leads her to Woland.

### Kenneth Fletcher

Kenneth Fletcher is the main protagonist.

He is a disciplined and intelligent detective with a strong ability to connect evidence and ask difficult questions. He also has unofficial contacts around the country who provide him with information and small cases outside normal police channels.

A year earlier, Kenneth's wife and five-year-old daughter were killed in the theatre attack.

Because the case was classified as terrorism, the investigation was taken away from ordinary detectives. Kenneth was never allowed to investigate his own family's deaths.

The loss has left him angry, exhausted, and deeply distrustful of institutions.

He used to care strongly about politics. Now the speeches of Diane Sterling and Edward Thorne sound like the same argument repeated from opposite sides.

What he wants is simple: he wants to know what really happened to his family.

### Professor Woland and His Entourage

A few days before the election, strange posters begin appearing throughout London:

**"PROFESSOR WOLAND & THE SEVENTH PROOF: AN EVENING OF THE IMPOSSIBLE"**

Woland is a world-famous foreign mentalist and illusionist. Almost nothing can be found about his real history.

He has been hired to perform a one-night-only gala at the Royal Alliance's flagship theatre on **election eve**.

His entourage each has an ordinary public role and a much stranger reality.

- **Koroviev** — Woland's tall, smiling tour manager and interpreter. He handles the paperwork, talks to everyone, and lies whenever necessary. His jokes often become reality.
- **Azazello** — Woland's head of security. Short, broad, red-haired, and physically intimidating. He handles situations that cannot be solved with words.
- **Hella** — Woland's personal assistant. Beautiful, calm, and deeply unsettling. She manages visitors and hospitality, but people who spend too much time alone with her often disappear.
- **Behemoth** — an enormous black cat who walks on two legs, drinks whisky, smokes cigars, and is presented to the public as part of the show. He is not a trained animal and is certainly not a pet.
- **Abaddon** — a pale, eyeless figure who is never introduced and never speaks. He appears only briefly: in a reflection, at the end of a corridor, or across a street just before someone dies. His appearances are a warning to the player.

### The Inciting Case

The story begins with an unofficial lead.

One Sunday evening, one of Kenneth's contacts tells him that **Arthur Pennington has disappeared**.

The informant says an anonymous caller reported Arthur missing and warned:

> "He will be disposed of soon."

Arthur is wealthy, powerful, and connected to one of the most important theatre organizations in London. The case could be worth a great deal of money.

But money is not the real reason Kenneth accepts.

When he hears the name **Royal Alliance**, he immediately thinks of the theatre attack that killed his family.

Kenneth decides to investigate Arthur's disappearance privately.

His first destination is Arthur's penthouse in **No. 50 Silver Tower, Godolphin Street**.

#### Hidden Canon

Kenneth does not know that Michael Astor is alive.

He does not know that the theatre attack was a false flag.

He believes Arthur is simply a missing businessman.

In reality, Arthur is the man who helped plan the attack that destroyed Kenneth's family.

The missing-person case is also a trap designed to bring Kenneth into the investigation.

# Act 1

## 1.1 The Call and the Tower

The act opens with a comic-book style sequence.

Late at night, **Frank Briggs**, one of Kenneth's unofficial contacts, calls him. Briggs tells Kenneth that Arthur Pennington has been missing for several days. He also mentions the anonymous warning that Arthur "will be disposed of soon."

Briggs gives Kenneth one unusual instruction: he must enter the Silver Tower at exactly **11:30 p.m.** At that time, the lobby staff and security guards will supposedly be gone for one hour.

Kenneth asks how Briggs could arrange this.

Briggs avoids the question.

Kenneth accepts the case, partly because he needs the money, but mainly because the name Royal Alliance reminds him of his wife and daughter.

#### Hidden Canon

The person speaking to Kenneth is not really Briggs.

It is **Behemoth**, imitating Briggs's voice.

The missing-person case is a trap created by Woland's group to bring Kenneth into the Silver Tower.

### Gameplay

Kenneth enters the Silver Tower lobby at 11:30 p.m.

The building looks as though everyone left only moments earlier. A half-finished cup of tea sits on a desk. A chair is still turned toward the reception area. A visitor log is open in the middle of a signature.

But the building is completely empty.

Kenneth wonders how Briggs managed to clear it.

To reach Arthur's penthouse, the player must solve two environmental puzzles:

1. Find the security code for the elevator.
2. Find the keycard that opens Arthur Pennington's penthouse.

Kenneth rides upward through the silent building and enters the penthouse.

## 1.2 The Penthouse

Arthur's penthouse is immaculate. It is modern, minimalist, expensive, and carefully arranged.

There is no sign of Arthur.

At first, Kenneth can explore only the public part of the apartment: the entrance hall and the home office.

Then he notices something strange.

Deep claw marks have been cut into the walls. They are small in the hallway but become much larger around one heavy reinforced door.

Most of the private wing is locked behind Arthur's biometric security system. Kenneth cannot open it yet.

Instead, he must search the two accessible rooms and examine what Arthur has left behind.

The main puzzle is a theatrical puzzle box disguised as an antique model stage. Solving it opens a hidden electric safe.

Inside the safe are documents connecting the Royal Alliance Theatre Association to the **One Soul Charity Foundation**. They also identify **Yusuf Tariq** as the director of One Soul.

The documents prove that Arthur and Yusuf are closely connected, but they do not yet explain the full purpose of that relationship.

Kenneth also finds several smaller clues:

- A business card marked with a single gold **"W"**, with no name or phone number.
- Signs that Arthur has been hiding relationships from more than one woman.
- An old payment record with the initials **"M.A / Final"** next to a press clipping about the theatre attack.

Kenneth assumes the payment may have been hush money given to a victim's family.

#### Hidden Canon

The "M.A / Final" payment is connected to **Michael Astor**.

At this point, Kenneth does not understand what it means.

His investigation now leads him to the **Royal Alliance Theatre's main building**.

## 1.3 Arthur: The Night It Happened

The story jumps back three nights.

The player now takes control of **Arthur Pennington**.

Arthur wakes in his dark bedroom, badly hungover. His phone is ringing.

The caller appears to be Zoey, his chief assistant.

She says that Arthur needs Professor Woland's number before an important meeting and insists that the information is in a contract they prepared that morning.

Arthur remembers neither Woland nor the contract.

He begins searching the penthouse for his briefcase.

#### Hidden Canon

The call is actually **Behemoth**, imitating Zoey's voice.

The real Zoey never called Arthur that night.

### Gameplay and Story

Arthur's private rooms reveal the truth behind his public image.

The luxury of the penthouse is excessive. The hidden rooms, private belongings, and locked objects show a man whose life is built around secrecy.

Arthur finds his briefcase.

Inside is a contract with Woland, signed in Arthur's own handwriting.

Arthur does not remember signing it.

The paper is strangely warm.

Then the penthouse begins to change.

Doors lock by themselves.

The lights flicker.

The rooms begin to show Arthur images of the theatre attack from one year earlier. Paintings seem to burn. Smoke and the smell of gunpowder fill the apartment. The screams of the dead come from behind the walls.

Arthur tries to deny what he is seeing.

He runs toward the bedroom.

There, near the window, he sees an enormous black cat standing upright, holding a glass of whisky and a cigar.

It is Behemoth.

The cat looks at Arthur.

Then it tears into the reinforced bedroom door, leaving the same claw marks Kenneth will find three nights later.

Arthur collapses.

The player now understands that Arthur is connected to the theatre attack.

The reason for the attack, and the full story of Michael Astor, remain hidden.

## 1.4 Margaret: Stranger in the Park

On the same rainy night, the story shifts to **Margaret Spenceley**.

She sits alone in Hyde Park. Around her, London is filled with election noise: rain, protesters, police sirens, and the competing voices of Diane Sterling and Edward Thorne.

Margaret has spent a year looking for Michael.

The political conflict around her means almost nothing to her.

A stranger appears and sits beside her.

The player controls Margaret during a dialogue scene. Her responses can be bitter, guarded, or hopeful. These choices shape her personality and how Woland responds to her, but they do not change the main story.

Woland asks her what she thinks about the election.

Then he starts talking.

He knows things about the political movements, their money, and their secret connections that an ordinary person should not know.

He exposes the hypocrisy behind the public conflict.

For this moment, the Devil is the most honest person in London.

Then Woland asks Margaret what she really wants.

She tells him about Michael.

She admits that everyone thinks he is dead, but she believes he is still alive.

Woland offers her a deal.

He will help her find Michael if she performs a series of tasks for him.

He does not tell her what the tasks are.

Margaret accepts.

#### Theme

Margaret represents **Love**: a sincere belief in another person even when the rest of the world says that belief is irrational.

Woland knows that this sincerity can be both powerful and dangerous.

# Act 2

## 2.1 The Royal Alliance

We return to the present.

Kenneth decides to investigate Arthur's office at the Royal Alliance Theatre.

He cannot enter openly, so he must find a way into the building after midnight.

When Kenneth reaches the management floor, the silence feels wrong.

There are no voices.

No servers.

No distant traffic.

No night watchman.

Then he sees blood on the carpet.

Kenneth follows the trail.

He finds deep scratches along the walls and long parallel marks that look too large to have been made by an ordinary animal.

### The Office

Kenneth enters Arthur's office and accesses his computer.

There he discovers the first major proof of the financial conspiracy.

Arthur and Yusuf are partners.

The Royal Alliance Theatre Association and the One Soul Charity Foundation receive millions through donations and other payments. The money is then moved through fake art commissions, cryptocurrency transactions, and other accounts before reaching political organizations.

The computer shows that both major political movements benefit from the same hidden network.

Kenneth also discovers a second secret.

Arthur has been involved with both **Zoey Fernandez** and **Rebecca Evans**, the theatre's financial manager. Private messages and emails reveal that neither woman fully understands the other relationship.

Kenneth leaves the office and follows another trail of blood.

It leads to the staff break room.

Inside, he finds **Rebecca's headless body**.

Her severed head has been placed on the counter.

The scene is impossible, grotesque, and strongly connected to the strange signs Kenneth has already seen in the building.

## 2.2 Zoey and Rebecca

We jump back to the previous night.

The player controls **Zoey Fernandez** inside the deserted Royal Alliance Theatre.

Arthur has been missing for days.

Zoey and Rebecca are trying to find him without attracting public attention.

Zoey is worried about Arthur's safety, but she is also afraid that his disappearance will expose their affair.

The player now learns something important: the phone call Arthur received earlier was not from Zoey.

Behemoth created the call.

The real Zoey never spoke to Arthur that night.

Rebecca is also secretly involved with Arthur.

Neither woman knew the full truth about the other until Arthur disappeared.

Rebecca says she will search the pubs and bars Arthur usually visits.

Once Rebecca leaves, Zoey becomes suspicious and begins investigating Rebecca herself.

### Gameplay

Zoey must bypass several security locks to enter Rebecca's office.

Inside, she finds evidence that Rebecca had also been seeing Arthur.

This makes Zoey angry, frightened, and increasingly unsure of what to believe.

When Zoey returns to her own office, Rebecca is already there.

She sits behind the desk without moving.

Her face is hidden in heavy shadow.

Zoey questions her about Arthur.

Rebecca says she found him drunk in a pub and sent him home in a taxi.

Zoey continues asking questions.

Rebecca's story begins to fall apart.

The details do not match.

Some of her answers become strange and impossible.

Finally, Rebecca moves closer to the light.

Her face is badly damaged, as though it has been beaten or crushed.

Yet she speaks normally, as if nothing is wrong.

Then the lights go out.

In the darkness, Zoey sees a huge hand reaching through the window.

Rebecca's head suddenly separates from her body and slides from her neck.

The room fills with a woman's scream.

A giant cat cries somewhere in the darkness.

Cut to black.
