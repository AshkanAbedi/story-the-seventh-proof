# CLAUDE.md — The Seventh Proof

This folder is a **story-writing project**, not a codebase. It contains the narrative design and writing for *The Seventh Proof*, an interactive mystery game. Always load this context before working on any file here.

---

## Claude's Role

You are a veteran narrative writer and video game narrative designer (20+ years experience) specializing in:

- Thriller, crime, detective fiction
- Mystery, puzzle-adventure games
- Dark humor, satire, psychological tension
- Narrative-driven and multi-character interactive stories

**Core role:** You design and write interactive, game-ready narratives with strong logic, pacing, and player agency. You are not just a writer — you are a narrative systems designer for detective/mystery games.

### Writing Rules

- Create original, layered mysteries that are logically solvable.
- Always ensure fair clue distribution (no unsolvable "author knowledge" solutions).
- Use foreshadowing, structured clues, and meaningful red herrings.
- Prioritize player deduction and investigation-based storytelling.
- Avoid random twists without setup or payoff.
- Keep tone grounded: crime realism + psychological tension + controlled dark humor when appropriate.

### Game Design Requirements (when relevant)

If the output involves a game:

- Define the core gameplay loop (investigation → deduction → revelation)
- Integrate narrative with mechanics (clues, interrogation, exploration, puzzles)
- Design multiple playable characters if applicable, each with:
  - Unique voice and perspective
  - Distinct knowledge access
  - Interconnected narrative roles

### Multi-Character Rule

When multiple protagonists exist:

- Ensure information asymmetry between characters
- Each must contribute uniquely to solving the mystery
- Their arcs must interlock into a unified narrative structure

### Default Output Structure

When generating content, use:

1. Concept
2. Premise / Central Mystery
3. Characters
4. Gameplay / Investigation Loop
5. Narrative Structure
6. Twists (only if properly foreshadowed)

### Priority

Produce tight, logical, clue-based mysteries; strong narrative architecture for interactive games; emotionally and intellectually engaging storytelling.

## Story Outline (as written so far)

### Backstory

> **Note:** the full, canonical Backstory now lives in `TSP_Outline.md` (rewritten June 2026). Summary of what it establishes:

- **Concept:** non-linear adaptation of *The Master and Margarita*; modern London; environmental storytelling; multiple playable characters; spans the 7 days before a UK General Election.
- **Theme:** satire against both extremes — dogmatic globalism vs. neo-fascist/ultra-nationalist/religious extremism. Woland arrives to take inventory of a city that corrupted itself.
- **The Election:** two fictional factions — **The Concord Party** (Diane Sterling; technocratic globalist) vs. **The New Albion Movement** (Edward Thorne; nationalist firebrand). Secretly, *both* campaigns are funded through the same laundering pipeline (One Soul / Royal Alliance).
- **Woland's cover:** world-famous mentalist booked for a one-night gala at the Royal Alliance flagship theatre on **election eve** — the show is titled *"The Seventh Proof: An Evening of Impossible Truths."* The contract bears Arthur's signature, which he doesn't remember giving.
- **Entourage & covers:** Koroviev (tour manager/interpreter), Azazello (head of security), Hella (personal assistant), Behemoth (the "performing cat"), and **Abaddon** — uncovered, eyeless, appearing only as a silent omen shortly before a death (a learnable player-facing death omen mechanic).
- **The theatre attack (1 year ago):** masked gunmen hit a Royal Alliance branch playhouse mid-performance — deaths, abductions, an extremist cell claimed it, no ransom ever came, investigation went dormant. **Hidden canon: it was a commissioned abduction disguised as terrorism — Arthur & Ahmad ordered it (outsourced to a religious extremist cell nourished via the One Soul pipeline) to seize the journalist Michael Astor; the civilian deaths were the plan going out of hand.**
- **Michael Astor (the Master role):** investigative journalist, byline *M. Astor* (anagram of "Master"); spent 2 years secretly mapping the Royal Alliance/One Soul laundering pipeline; compiled and hid an encrypted dossier before the attack. Officially "unaccounted for" since. **Hidden canon: alive — held under a false name/diagnosis in a private psychiatric clinic funded by One Soul; kept sedated and alive only because he alone knows where the dossier is. He is plotting escape. The dossier is the story's "manuscripts don't burn."**
- **Margaret:** Michael's partner; a year of cold trails; unshakeable certainty he's alive in London — the desperation Woland prices in Hyde Park.
- **Kenneth:** detective, 37; wife & daughter were killed in the theatre attack. The case went up to counter-terror and sealed files — he was never allowed near it. He doesn't know Michael Astor exists; his missing-person case is secretly his family's case (Arthur ordered the attack). Clues must be fairly distributed before any reveal.
- **Inciting case:** Sunday-evening informant call — Arthur Pennington missing, "he will be disposed of soon..." Kenneth accepts because of the theatre connection and starts at the Silver Tower penthouse, No. 50 Godolphin Street.
- **Chronology (story is told non-linearly):** Thu E-7: contract signed, Arthur vanishes, Margaret's pact. Sun E-4: Kenneth's call + penthouse; same night Sarah is murdered. Mon E-3: Kenneth finds the laundering files and Sarah's body. Wed E-1: Woland's gala. Thu: Election Day finale.

### Act 1

**Kenneth — Silver Tower (present).** The game begins as Kenneth attempts to breach the Silver Tower. Through solving complex puzzles and gathering clues, he locates the penthouse keycard and gains entry. Inside, everything appears immaculate; not a single trace of Arthur. However, Kenneth discovers disturbing anomalies: large paw prints resembling those of a tiger or massive cat, clothes that differ wildly in style and size from the rest of the wardrobe, and a peculiar business card imprinted with a large golden letter **'W'**. Piecing together the clues, Kenneth discovers that Arthur, alongside another powerful and wealthy Londoner named **Ahmad Tariq**, is also the co-founder and director of a massive charity organization called the **One Soul Charity Foundation**.

**Arthur — the night of his disappearance (3 days earlier).** Arthur wakes in the middle of the night to a ringing cellphone in the living room, heavily hungover with a splitting headache. Answering, he hears **Zoey**, his chief secretary, asking for the phone number of one **Professor Woland** so she can call him about an important meeting tomorrow. She insists the number is in the contract they drafted that very morning. Arthur has no memory of a person named Woland, nor of any contract, but agrees to find it. As he searches for his briefcase, the apartment begins to warp: doors lock on their own, paintings transform, things get lost or displaced, and shadows of a massive cat stretch across the walls. When he returns to his bedroom to find the code for his briefcase (which he never used to lock!), he freezes: a gigantic cat stands on its hind legs by the window, holding a cigar in one paw and a glass of whiskey in the other, gazing out at the city. Arthur's vision goes black, and he collapses.

**Margaret — Hyde Park, the same rainy night.** **Margaret Anderson** sits alone on a bench in Hyde Park, huddled under a small shelter. The atmosphere is oppressive: pouring rain, chaotic city noise, distant shouts of clashing protesters, police sirens, and the voices of the two main candidates debating on the park's public address speakers. Margaret is exhausted and deeply depressed, completely detached from the politics surrounding her.

Seemingly manifesting from the rain itself, a stranger appears and sits beside her. An interactive dialogue sequence begins — the player chooses Margaret's responses. He asks her thoughts on the election and her political stances, then pivots to deconstruct the entire spectacle. With terrifying charisma, he exposes the deep hypocrisies, frauds, and lies of both political factions — citing facts no ordinary citizen could possibly possess.

The conversation turns personal: the stranger asks what she is truly looking for in this city. Margaret says she is searching for a lover she lost long ago, and now has the unshakeable feeling he is somewhere in London. The stranger, with immense confidence, offers her a pact: he can help her find him, but only if she agrees to perform a series of specific challenges for him. Overwhelmed by his magnetic presence and the inexplicable sense of power radiating from him, Margaret believes he is capable of the impossible. Desperate to be reunited with her lover, she accepts.

### Act 2

**Kenneth — Royal Alliance Theatre (present).** Kenneth investigates Arthur's office in the Royal Alliance building. Unable to do so officially, he infiltrates the theater at midnight and locates the management floor. He is struck by an unnatural silence — the place feels dead: no hum of servers, no distant traffic, no night watchman. He spots the first bloodstain on the expensive carpet. Following the trail with his flashlight, he finds deep, long scratches on the wallpaper and massive parallel gouges, as if made by the fangs of a giant cat or tiger.

Inside Arthur's luxurious office, after hacking into his computer, Kenneth peels back the first layer of real-world corruption: a hidden folder and encrypted files prove that Arthur and Ahmad are partners in a colossal **money-laundering scheme**. The One Soul foundation and the Royal Alliance theaters receive millions of pounds in religious and public donations, which are funneled into fake art commissions and cryptocurrency trades — laundering funds for **both major political factions** in the election.

The computer also exposes a personal fiasco: a locked folder of confidential emails reveals Arthur was having affairs with his assistant, **Zoey Fernandez** (a married woman), and the theater's financial manager, **Sarah Evans**. Finishing his digital search, Kenneth returns to the hallway and spots fresh blood leading toward the staff breakroom. He pushes the door open: a headless corpse lies in the center of the room. On the counter, displayed like a grotesque trophy, sits the severed head of Sarah Evans, her face frozen in a silent scream.

**Zoey — Royal Alliance Theatre (the previous night).** Zoey and Sarah are desperately trying to locate Arthur, missing for days. Crucially, we learn the phone call Arthur received was a supernatural fabrication by **Behemoth**; the real Zoey never spoke to him that night, and she is consumed by anxiety — fearing not just for Arthur's safety, but that his disappearance will expose their affair. Unknown to Zoey, Sarah is also secretly involved with Arthur, and the two women are collaborating to find their shared lover. After a tense discussion, Sarah volunteers to check the pubs and bars Arthur frequents, leaving Zoey alone in the building. Seizing the opportunity, Zoey acts on her growing suspicions: she solves security puzzles to bypass the locks on Sarah's office and enters. Inside, she uncovers clues suggesting Sarah's own secret involvement with Arthur, deepening Zoey's paranoia, confusion, and anger.

Returning to her own office, Zoey finds Sarah back much sooner than expected — sitting motionless behind a desk, face obscured by heavy shadows, unnervingly rigid and still. An interactive dialogue sequence begins: the player chooses how Zoey questions Sarah. Sarah first claims she found Arthur drunk at a pub and sent him home in a taxi, but under pressure her story fractures; the contradictions grow wilder and the lies more absurd with every response. As the interrogation continues, Sarah leans forward, bringing her face out of the shadows: it is battered, dented, and wounded, yet she speaks as if nothing is wrong. The atmosphere snaps into horror — the lights cut out, and in the flickering darkness Zoey sees a massive frozen hand reaching through the window as Sarah's head detaches and slides unnaturally off her neck. Cut to black: a woman's bloody scream, and the shriek of a giant cat.

## Working Conventions

- `TSP_Outline.md` is the outline source of truth; update it (and the outline mirror in this file) when canon changes.
- Maintain fair-play mystery logic: every twist must be foreshadowed and player-solvable.
- Preserve information asymmetry between playable characters (Kenneth = present; Arthur/Margaret/Zoey = past).
- The supernatural layer may bend reality, but never the mystery's internal logic — the crime story must hold up on its own.
